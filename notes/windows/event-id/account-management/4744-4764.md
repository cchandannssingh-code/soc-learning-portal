# Distribution Group Management Events (4744–4764)

## Overview

Distribution Group Management events record changes made to **security-disabled Active Directory groups** (Distribution Groups). These groups are primarily used for **email distribution** and **organizational communication** rather than authorization or access control.

Unlike security groups, **distribution groups do not grant permissions** to resources.

---

## Why It Matters

Although these events are generally considered **low security priority**, they can be useful during investigations involving:

* Microsoft Exchange administration
* Email distribution list modifications
* Insider threats
* Business Email Compromise (BEC)
* Unauthorized changes to executive, HR, finance, or sensitive mailing lists

---

## Event IDs

### Security-Disabled Local Group

* **4744** – Group created
* **4745** – Group changed
* **4746** – Member added
* **4747** – Member removed
* **4748** – Group deleted

### Security-Disabled Global Group

* **4749** – Group created
* **4750** – Group changed
* **4751** – Member added
* **4752** – Member removed
* **4753** – Group deleted

### Security-Disabled Universal Group

* **4759** – Group created
* **4760** – Group changed
* **4761** – Member added
* **4762** – Member removed
* **4763** – Group deleted

### Group Type Change

* **4764** – Group type changed (for example, Security Group ↔ Distribution Group or group scope modified)

---

## SOC Relevance

**Priority:** ⭐⭐☆☆☆ (Low)

Most organizations generate these events during normal administrative activities. They are typically not associated with privilege escalation because distribution groups do not provide access permissions.

However, analysts should review these events when:

* Sensitive mailing lists are modified.
* Unexpected users are added to executive or finance distribution lists.
* Investigating Exchange or Microsoft 365 incidents.
* Correlating with phishing or Business Email Compromise investigations.

---

## Investigation Tips

When reviewing these events, verify:

* Who performed the change?
* Which distribution group was modified?
* Which user was added or removed?
* Was the activity expected and approved?
* Are there related authentication, Exchange, or email security alerts?

---

## Detection Ideas

Generate alerts for:

* Changes to high-profile distribution groups (Executive, HR, Finance).
* Large numbers of distribution group modifications in a short period.
* Changes performed by unusual or non-administrative accounts.
* Distribution group modifications occurring outside normal business hours.

---

## SOC Analyst Summary

Distribution Group Management events are primarily **administrative auditing events**. While they are not commonly used to detect privilege escalation, they can provide valuable evidence during email security, insider threat, and Exchange-related investigations. Analysts should prioritize these events only when they involve sensitive distribution lists or correlate with other suspicious activity.
